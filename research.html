RESEARCH PROGRAM

Research

My research uses vascular function, and endothelial dysfunction in particular, as a lens for understanding disease. I focus on RAAS–eNOS signaling in Long COVID and obstructive sleep apnea, and on exercise-based interventions that may improve vascular health.

AREA 01

Endothelial dysfunction and RAAS–eNOS signaling

The endothelium regulates vascular tone, in part through nitric oxide produced by endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS). This work examines how signaling through the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) and eNOS relates to impaired endothelial function, and how these pathways differ across disease states.

AREA 02

Long COVID

Through the NIH RECOVER initiative, I contribute to research on the long-term consequences of SARS-CoV-2 infection, with an emphasis on vascular function and the biological pathways that may underlie persistent symptoms.

AREA 03

Obstructive sleep apnea and vascular health

Obstructive sleep apnea is closely linked to hypertension and cardiovascular risk. My work investigates endothelial dysfunction in people with sleep apnea, including the CHART clinical trial of inspiratory muscle training in adults with above-normal blood pressure and sleep apnea.

AREA 04

Inspiratory muscle strength training

Inspiratory muscle strength training (IMST) is a brief, high-resistance, low-volume breathing exercise. I study its effects on blood pressure, endothelial function, and cerebrovascular function, including trials in sleep apnea (CHART) and type 2 diabetes (DIT), and the dose needed to maintain its benefits.
